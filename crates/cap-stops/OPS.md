# stops

Every stop as a list of dated events, oldest first — planned, permitted, built, in_service, suspended, moved, changed, retired (transit-stops; D-153, D-157). A stop has a life of its own: its stages go forward (planned, permitted, built, in service — a stage may be skipped), a stop in service may be suspended for a while (a closure, works, an event — expected to return, and it may not), and it ends retired. Nothing in the document means "today": a stop on a date is its events applied in order up to that date, and a temporary suspension or move (until) applies only while it lasts, so the earlier state returns on its until date. Every detail — the point, name, code, access, fare zone, amenities (D-154: true there, false checked and not there, absent not recorded), the site survey's answers and the stop's references to other systems (D-158, D-160) — changes only by an event. A correction edits the event that was wrong; a real change adds one. The stop's id is its lasting key, kept through a move (D-145). A stops document registers on the kernel's load_stops, refused unless validate would pass it. A pick only reads the document (D-157).

## validate

What a write must pass: every refusal, each naming its stop, the event's index in the stop's list, and its problem — no events; an unknown event type; a date that is not YYYY-MM-DD; a missing date (only a first in_service may go undated); events out of date order; a stop not beginning at a stage (planned, permitted, built, in_service); an event after retired; a planned, permitted, or built when the stop is already at or past that stage on its date (stage_back); a suspended when the stop is not in service on its date (not_in_service); an in_service when it already is (already_in_service); an until on or before its event's date; a field its event type does not set (until only on suspended and moved, the point only on a stage event and moved, details only on a stage event and changed, replaces only on a stage event); a missing or half point (required of a stop's first event and of a move); a point off the earth; a surveyed_by on an event that sets no details; a core site question answered outside its kind (bad_answer: an answer not on its list, or a count that is not a whole number — the agency's own questions are not checked); a consolidation naming the stop itself or a stop the document does not hold. An event out of date order is refused for that alone. With no refusal, answers the canonical document — stops by id, each stop's events as written, every field this repo does not know kept — and its document_hash (the module is the hash authority: the same hash load_stops answers). A document whose schema_version this build does not read is an error.

## stops_on

Every stop as its events make it on the date, in id order — or only stop_ids, where given, and only those at statuses, where given: its status (planned, permitted, built, in_service, suspended, retired), when that status began (since; none for a stop in service from before any record), when it ends (until, a temporary one: its until, or sooner the next status event after the date — a stop back early, or retired while suspended), the note on the event that set it, when it was last surveyed and by whom (surveyed_on, surveyed_by), its point, and its details, amenities, site answers, and references. Beside them, counts: the stops on the date by status (every one, whatever stop_ids and statuses ask), only the statuses some stop has. A stop not yet there on the date (its first event is later) is not listed. Read over a document registered by load_stops.

## stop

One stop on the date — null before it exists — and every event it has, oldest first: the card's record and its history. Beside them, next: what the stop may become that day — the lifecycle events (planned, permitted, built, in_service, suspended, retired) a write dated that day would accept, in that order; a move or a change of details is always a choice while it is not retired. A stop the document does not hold is an error.

## seed

A stops document from a registered network's stops: each stop one in_service event with no date (no feed records when a stop went into service), carrying its point and every detail — registered, answered with its stops_hash.

## add_event

One edit: the document with `event` added to stop `stop_id` in its place by date — after every event dated on or before it (an undated first event first), before every later one, every other event in its order; an id the document does not hold begins a stop. Then checked as a write is (validate): every refusal, each naming its stop, event, and problem, and no document; or none, and the document registered and answered with its stops_hash. A page writes the answer through the deployment's receive path (POST /stops), which checks it again.

## correct_event

One correction: a correction edits the event that was wrong (D-153; the store keeps the earlier version). The document with event `index` (its place in the stop's list, from 0) of stop `stop_id` taken out and `event` placed by date as add_event places one — the same event in its place changes nothing — or, with no `event`, taken out. Then checked as a write is: every refusal and no document, or none and the document registered and answered with its stops_hash. A stop or index the document does not hold is an error.

## read_records

What a pick reads of the stops (D-157): the registered network with each stop's record — its point, name, code, station, and details (amenities, site answers, and references are the stops document's alone) — as the stops document has it on `on`, the pick's first date. A stop the document does not hold, or that is not there yet that day, keeps the network's own record; nothing else in the network changes, and the stops document is never written. A network read from a document seeded from it comes back under the same hash. Registered, answered with the network and its network_hash.

## visit_warnings

What the planner is told of the stops the network's patterns visit over its period, from `from` until `until` (exclusive; open-ended without): each span a stop is not in service — planned, permitted, built, retired, or not there yet (no status) — and each span it is suspended when the suspension covers the whole period or has no end (a shorter suspension is real time's to say, not the pick's). Each warning is `stop_not_in_service` with the stop, its status, and the span, in stop order. Warnings, never refusals; a stop the document does not hold says nothing.

## export_versions

GTFS out with the stops' history (D-151): the registered network over its dates, from `from` until `until` (exclusive; open-ended without), for cap-network's export. A stop whose record — point, name, code, details — changes inside the dates is one stop per version: the version in force on the last date under the stop's id, each earlier one under `<id>@<its first date>` (the first one's is `from`), each with its own record (so the code the stop keeps is shared). Each pattern visiting such a stop splits at those dates: for each earlier version's dates a copy of the pattern (`<pattern>@<date>`) and of its trips (`<trip>@<date>`) under the service type cut to those dates (`<type>@<first date>..<last date>`), the last dates keeping the pattern's and trips' own ids under the type cut from their first date (`<type>@<first date>`); two patterns splitting at different dates never share a cut type; each block runs a trip's copies beside it (they never share a day). On every date the export runs exactly the trips the pick runs that day, at the stops' records of that day; before `from` the first version runs, from `until` on the last. A suspension or a retirement changes no record, so splits nothing; with no record changing, the answer is read_records on `from`. Registered, answered with the network and its network_hash.

## import_events

GTFS in with the stops' history (D-150): a feed's stops — the network its import answered — compared with the stops document on `on`, the feed's first service date (omitted: the feed's own — the earliest date any of its service runs — answered as `on`), each event it records naming the feed (`from_feed`) when `feed` (the feed's id, its hash) is given, each difference an event dated that day: `in_service` (with the feed's point and details) for a stop the document lacks or has not yet in service (planned, permitted, built, not there yet); `moved` for another point; `changed` for the details that differ (a detail the feed does not carry is no difference; amenities, site answers, and references are the document's alone). A suspended stop the feed lists stays suspended: a suspension is real time's. A made id (`<id>@<date>`, export_versions) is a version of its stop, and the version in force on `on` is compared: the made version with the latest date on or before `on`, else the stop's own id (a feed does not say where its own id's version begins, so `on` must be the feed's first service date) — so a re-import of this repo's own export adds no event. Every event already recorded stands. Answers the next document (registered, with its stops_hash), each event added (`added`: stop and event), the stops the document has in service or suspended on `on` that the feed does not list (`unlisted`, which keep their events — a stop planned, permitted, built, retired, or not there yet is expected missing from a feed, and is not named), and the stops whose difference would break their history (`unapplied`: nothing is added for them). The caller writes the document through the receive path.

## questions

What a site survey asks (D-158): the core site questions in the order C-TRAN's Service Stop Worksheet asks them, each with the kind of answer it takes — one_of (its answers listed), number (a whole number), or text — and the core amenity names (D-154). An answer is recorded in an event's site, by question; a part's id in its refs (sign, tms, hp, gis, shelter, …); who surveyed in its surveyed_by. The agency's own questions are kept and not checked. Reads no document.

## attribute_surveys

The surveyor is the signed-in writer (D-158), as the receive path takes a write: `stops` with every survey event (one carrying surveyed_by — a name, or empty to leave it to the writer) that the held document (`stops_hash`, registered by load_stops; absent for a first write) does not hold, compared whole in the same stop, naming `by` — whatever name the client sent, and a held survey the client rewrote included. Every event the held document holds keeps its surveyor; nothing else changes; attributing twice is attributing once. Answers the document unchecked: validate follows. POST /stops runs it with `by` the configured claim of the verified token (`-oidc-name-claim`).

## feed_events

What the planner is shown of an import (D-150): the events the document holds that the feed `feed` (its id, the hash import_events named) recorded, each with its stop — in stop order, each stop's in its order. A feed that recorded nothing answers none.
