# board

The board: when does service leave this stop — at a stop (a station pin or bay with nothing of its own answers its pooled walking group), realtime-first over the live document the rt capability mints (its predictions and its service alerts), with what the in-force edits took away from the stop; the same stop grouped by route and destination for the display at it; or for a personal saved set (the watchlist document, the same question parameterized by a personal document). Every answer is the materialized day over the NETWORK document, patched with the in-force edits. Model-native end to end; the domain code lives in the shared transit-dispatch library. Assemble for anyone who reads a departure board.

## stop_board

THE board at one stop on a service date: the network's materialized day with the in-force edits (edits_hash), REALTIME-FIRST when live_hash names a registered live document — rows ordered by when the bus actually leaves, a late bus scheduled before from_secs that has not left is still on the board, an early one that already left is not, and each row carries expected_secs, delay_secs (against the board's own patched time), and status under the live document's window (no_prediction without live data). A departure the live document says does not happen — its trip cancelled, or this stop skipped — is no row: it leaves the board silently (D-73). Each row names its route (route_name, route_long_name), the stop it leaves from (stop, stop_name — the bay, on a pooled board — and platform, the bay's own identifier, GTFS platform_code), and its destination as the sign says at that visit (the visit's headsign, else the trip's, else the final stop's station). A trip's final visit is an arrival, never a row. skipped: the scheduled departures the in-force edits took away from the stop, each with the diversion that skips it or the cancel that took its trip (a trip the edit document cancels is never a row — unlike the live feed's own cancellation, it is listed here, D-100) — why a stop is emptier than its schedule. alerts: the live document's service alerts about the board (a row, a skipped visit, or the stop itself); each row and skipped visit names the ids about it. Each row says how long until it leaves (wait, from from_secs to when it actually leaves): due under a minute, minutes (rounded down) under an hour, else the clock time (secs). A stop with nothing of its own — nothing leaving, nothing skipped — that belongs to a walking group (a station pin) answers the group's board with pooled: true, each row naming its bay; a plain stop never pools.

## grouped_board

THE board grouped for the display at a stop: one group per route AND destination the stop is served by on the date — its whole day, so a group whose last departure has left stays on the board with departures: [] (the route has stopped here today) and next: when it runs here next — {date, days_ahead (1 = tomorrow), weekday ("Monday"), departure_secs, stop} — the earliest departure of that route to that destination on the first of the next seven days whose day (patched by that date's in-force edits; never the live document) carries one, counted only if it leaves at or after from_secs; null with departures left, or none within the week — each holding its next per_group departures (default 3) by stop_board's realtime-first rule (the same rows, with wait, status, and the bay), and skipped: the visits the in-force edits took from that group, each with its diversion or its cancel (a cancelled trip takes none of the group's next departures). Groups list in the route's display order (route_sort_order, lower first; routes without one after them, by id), then by destination, and carry the route's names and authored colors. alerts: the live document's alerts about the stop or any departure still to leave it. A stop with nothing of its own all day that belongs to a walking group (a station pin) pools the group, pooled: true, each row naming its bay. Uncut (a per_group as large as the day), the groups' rows together are stop_board's rows.

## watchlist_board

The board for a watchlist: each watched stop's board on a service date, labelled per the document's version — the same question the per-stop board answers, parameterized by the personal document, so the same answer: each stop's `board` is `stop_board` with the same edits (in force, skipped visits listed) and `live_hash` (realtime-first), and a watched station pin pools its bays.
